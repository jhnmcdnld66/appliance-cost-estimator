How much does a space heater cost to run?

Most plug-in electric space heaters sold in the U.S. use up to 1500 watts on their high setting. That number is set by the limits of a standard household outlet, so a small heater and a large one often draw the same power on high.

The quick math

1500 watts is 1.5 kilowatts (kW). Run it for one hour and it uses 1.5 kilowatt-hours (kWh) of electricity. Multiply by your electric rate to get the cost:

  • At $0.17 per kWh (roughly the U.S. average): 1.5 × $0.17 = about $0.26 per hour.
  • 8 hours a day: 12 kWh, or about $2.04 per day.
  • 8 hours a day for 30 days: 360 kWh, or about $61 per month.

Your rate matters a lot. The same heater running the same hours costs about $43 a month at $0.12 per kWh and about $108 a month at $0.30 per kWh. Here's how to find your rate.

Why your real cost is usually lower

Most heaters have a thermostat. Once the room warms up, the heater switches off, then back on when the room cools. If it only runs half the time, the cost is about half. In the calculator, use the percent of time running field for this. A heater in a drafty room on a cold night may run nearly nonstop; one in a small, well-insulated room may run much less.

The low setting on many heaters is around 750–900 watts, which roughly halves the cost per hour compared with high.

Are some space heaters more efficient?

For standard electric resistance heaters, not really. Ceramic, oil-filled, and fan-forced models all turn nearly 100% of the electricity they use into heat. A 1500-watt heater gives off about the same heat whichever style it is. Features like a good thermostat, a timer, or an eco mode can cut costs because they stop the heater from running more than it needs to. A heat pump is the exception: it moves heat instead of making it, so it can deliver more heat per kWh.

Ways to spend less

  • Heat only the room you're using, and close the door.
  • Turn the main thermostat down a few degrees while you use the space heater, so you aren't paying to heat the whole house too.
  • Use the thermostat and timer instead of running it on high all the time.
  • Seal drafts around windows and doors.

Safety reminders

Plug space heaters directly into a wall outlet, not an extension cord or power strip. Keep them at least 3 feet from anything that can burn, and pick a model with tip-over and overheat shut-off. Never leave one running while you sleep or when no one is home.
